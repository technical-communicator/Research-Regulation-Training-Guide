Common Rule

United States

45 CFR Part 46: Federal Policy for the Protection of Human Subjects

Authority: Office for Human Research Protections (OHRP) & 19 Federal Departments/Agencies
Effective Date (2018 Revisions): January 21, 2019
Official Source: HHS OHRP Website

Overview

The Common Rule is the baseline standard of ethics by which any government-funded research in the US is held. It was originally published in 1991 and underwent significant revisions in 2018 (effective January 2019).

The Common Rule has been adopted by 20 federal departments and agencies, ensuring consistent protections for human research subjects across the federal government. It provides a uniform set of requirements for human subjects research conducted, supported, or otherwise subject to regulation by these agencies.

2018 Final Rule: The revised Common Rule includes important updates to informed consent requirements, exempt research categories, and single IRB review for multi-site studies.

Structure: Subparts A through D

Subpart A: Basic HHS Policy for Protection of Human Research Subjects

Core requirements applicable to all human subjects research, including:

  • IRB review and approval requirements
  • Informed consent requirements
  • Exempt research categories
  • Expedited review procedures
  • Criteria for IRB approval

Subpart B: Additional Protections for Pregnant Women, Human Fetuses, and Neonates

Enhanced safeguards for research involving:

  • Pregnant women or fetuses
  • Neonates of uncertain viability
  • Nonviable neonates
  • After delivery (placenta, dead fetus, fetal material)

Subpart C: Additional Protections for Prisoners

Special requirements for research involving prisoners:

  • Composition of IRB reviewing prisoner research
  • Additional duties of IRB
  • Permitted categories of prisoner research
  • Additional approval requirements from Secretary HHS

Subpart D: Additional Protections for Children

Enhanced protections for research involving children:

  • Parental permission requirements
  • Child assent when capable
  • Risk/benefit categories for research
  • Conditions for waiving assent

Key Requirements

IRB Review (§46.109)

  • Research must be reviewed and approved by an IRB
  • IRB must conduct continuing review at least annually
  • IRB may suspend or terminate approval

Informed Consent (§46.116, §46.117)

General Requirements:

  • Begin with concise and focused presentation of key information
  • Obtain consent in language understandable to the subject
  • Provide sufficient opportunity to consider participation
  • Minimize possibility of coercion or undue influence

Required Elements:

  • Statement that study involves research
  • Purpose, duration, procedures, and identification of experimental procedures
  • Reasonably foreseeable risks
  • Benefits to subject or others
  • Appropriate alternative procedures or courses of treatment
  • Confidentiality provisions
  • Compensation and treatment for injuries (if more than minimal risk)
  • Contacts for questions and rights
  • Statement that participation is voluntary

Criteria for IRB Approval (§46.111)

To approve research, the IRB must determine that:

  • Risks are minimized
  • Risks are reasonable in relation to anticipated benefits
  • Selection of subjects is equitable
  • Informed consent will be sought and documented
  • Data monitoring is adequate when appropriate
  • Privacy and confidentiality are protected
  • Additional safeguards for vulnerable subjects

2018 Revisions (Final Rule)

Major Changes

Informed Consent Improvements

  • New requirement to begin with concise and focused key information
  • Prohibition of exculpatory language clarified
  • New requirements for posting clinical trial consent forms
  • New element: whether biospecimens will be used for commercial profit and whether subjects will share in that profit

Exempt Research Categories Expanded

  • Expanded exempt categories from 6 to 8
  • Created "limited IRB review" for certain exempt categories
  • New category for benign behavioral interventions
  • New category for secondary research with broad consent
  • Expanded storage and maintenance of identifiable information

Single IRB for Multi-Site Research

  • Required use of single IRB for cooperative research
  • Applies to federally funded studies conducted at more than one site
  • Exceptions allowed in limited circumstances

Continuing Review Changes

  • Eliminated requirement for continuing review for certain minimal risk studies
  • Eliminated continuing review for studies where data analysis is only remaining activity

Exempt Research Categories (§46.104)

The following categories are exempt from most Common Rule requirements (limited IRB review may still be required for some):

Category 1: Educational Settings ▼

Research conducted in established or commonly accepted educational settings, involving normal educational practices.

Category 2: Educational Tests, Surveys, Interviews ▼

Research involving educational tests, survey procedures, interview procedures, or observation of public behavior (with conditions).

Category 3: Benign Behavioral Interventions ▼

Research involving benign behavioral interventions with adult subjects who prospectively agree (requires limited IRB review).

Category 4: Secondary Research ▼

Secondary research uses of identifiable private information or biospecimens (with conditions).

Note: Categories 5-8 cover public benefit/service programs, taste/food quality, and storage/maintenance of data. See official regulations for complete details.
