Overview

The FDA regulations in 21 CFR Parts 50 and 56 establish requirements for protecting human subjects in clinical investigations of FDA-regulated products. These regulations apply to studies of drugs, biologics, and medical devices intended for submission to the FDA.

Part 50 focuses on informed consent requirements and protections for human subjects, while Part 56 establishes standards for Institutional Review Board (IRB) organization, composition, and operation.

Note: These regulations work in conjunction with the Common Rule (45 CFR 46) for federally-funded research and may overlap with other federal and state requirements.

21 CFR Part 50: Protection of Human Subjects

Purpose and Scope

21 CFR Part 50 establishes standards for informed consent and protections for human subjects participating in clinical investigations regulated by the FDA. It applies to all clinical investigations involving products subject to FDA jurisdiction.

Key Requirements

Informed Consent (§50.20)

No investigator may involve a human being as a subject in research unless the investigator has obtained the legally effective informed consent of the subject or the subject's legally authorized representative.

General Requirements for Informed Consent (§50.20)

  • Consent must be obtained before the subject's participation
  • Consent process must give the subject or representative sufficient opportunity to consider participation
  • Information must be in language understandable to the subject
  • No exculpatory language may be included
  • Consent may not involve coercion or undue influence

Elements of Informed Consent (§50.25)

Basic Elements:

  • A statement that the study involves research
  • Explanation of the purposes of the research
  • Expected duration of subject's participation
  • Description of procedures to be followed
  • Identification of experimental procedures
  • Description of foreseeable risks or discomforts
  • Description of potential benefits
  • Disclosure of alternative procedures or treatments
  • Statement about confidentiality of records
  • For research involving more than minimal risk, explanation of compensation and treatment available if injury occurs
  • Explanation of whom to contact for questions
  • Statement that participation is voluntary and refusal involves no penalty

Additional Elements (when appropriate):

  • Statement that treatment or procedure may involve unforeseeable risks
  • Circumstances under which participation may be terminated
  • Additional costs to the subject
  • Consequences of subject's decision to withdraw
  • Statement about significant new findings
  • Approximate number of subjects involved

Documentation of Informed Consent (§50.27)

  • Must be documented by a written consent form approved by the IRB
  • Form must be signed and dated by the subject or legally authorized representative
  • Copy must be given to the person signing the form

Special Protections for Children (Subpart D)

  • Requires assent from children capable of providing it
  • Requires permission from parents or guardians
  • Categorizes research by risk level and potential benefit
  • Additional safeguards for vulnerable pediatric populations

Exception from Informed Consent (§50.23, §50.24)

Limited exceptions exist for:

  • Emergency research when consent cannot be obtained and certain conditions are met (§50.24)
  • Specific situations outlined in §50.23

21 CFR Part 56: Institutional Review Boards

Purpose and Scope

21 CFR Part 56 establishes requirements for the organization, composition, function, operation, and responsibilities of Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) that review biomedical research involving human subjects.

Key Requirements

IRB Composition (§56.107)

  • At least five members with varying backgrounds
  • Members must be sufficiently qualified through experience and expertise
  • Must include at least one member whose primary concerns are in scientific areas
  • Must include at least one member whose primary concerns are in nonscientific areas
  • Must include at least one member who is not affiliated with the institution
  • No IRB may consist entirely of men, women, or members of one profession
  • May not have a member participate in review of research in which they have a conflicting interest

IRB Functions and Operations (§56.108, §56.109)

  • Must follow written procedures for conducting initial and continuing review
  • Must follow written procedures for reporting findings and actions to investigators and institution
  • Must follow written procedures for determining which projects require review more often than annually
  • Must follow written procedures for ensuring prompt reporting to IRB of changes in research
  • Quorum requirements: majority of members must be present, including at least one non-scientist
  • Approval requires majority vote of members present

IRB Review Criteria (§56.111)

To approve research, the IRB must determine that:

  • Risks to subjects are minimized
  • Risks are reasonable in relation to anticipated benefits and knowledge to be gained
  • Selection of subjects is equitable
  • Informed consent will be sought and documented (with limited exceptions)
  • Data monitoring plan is adequate when appropriate
  • Privacy and confidentiality protections are adequate
  • Additional safeguards for vulnerable subjects when appropriate

Continuing Review (§56.109(f))

  • IRB must conduct continuing review at intervals appropriate to the degree of risk
  • Review must occur at least once per year
  • IRB has authority to observe or have third party observe consent process and research

Expedited Review (§56.110)

  • Certain categories of research may be reviewed by expedited procedures
  • Research must present no more than minimal risk
  • IRB chairperson or designated experienced reviewer(s) may review
  • May not disapprove research; must go to full board

IRB Records and Reports (§56.115)

IRB must maintain:

  • Copies of all research proposals reviewed, approved sample consent documents, progress reports, and reports of injuries
  • Minutes of IRB meetings with attendance, actions taken, votes, basis for requiring changes, and dissenting opinions
  • Records of continuing review activities
  • Records of all correspondence between IRB and investigators
  • Membership roster identifying members
  • Written procedures and statements of significant new findings
  • Records must be retained for at least 3 years after completion of research

Applicability

When FDA Regulations Apply

21 CFR Parts 50 and 56 apply to clinical investigations regulated by the FDA, including:

  • Drug Studies: Investigations of drugs and biologics subject to IND or NDA requirements
  • Device Studies: Investigations of medical devices subject to IDE requirements or premarket approval
  • Food Additive Studies: Certain studies of food additives
  • Color Additive Studies: Certain studies of color additives

Relationship to Other Regulations

Common Rule (45 CFR 46)

For federally-funded research, both FDA regulations and the Common Rule may apply. When both apply, the more stringent requirements must be followed. Many institutions use a single IRB to review under both sets of regulations.
