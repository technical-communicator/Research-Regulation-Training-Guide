HIPAA Privacy Rule

United States

45 CFR Parts 160 & 164: Standards for Privacy of Individually Identifiable Health Information

Authority:Department of Health and Human Services (HHS)
Effective Date:April 14, 2003 (Privacy Rule)
Official Source:HHS HIPAA Website

Overview

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Privacy Rule establishes national standards to protect individuals' medical records and other individually identifiable health information (Protected Health Information or PHI). The rule applies to covered entities and their business associates.

For research, HIPAA creates specific requirements for the use and disclosure of PHI, with several pathways for compliance.

Protected Health Information (PHI)

PHI includes individually identifiable health information transmitted or maintained in any form that:

  • Relates to past, present, or future physical or mental health or condition
  • Relates to provision of health care
  • Relates to past, present, or future payment for health care
  • Identifies the individual or could be used to identify the individual

18 HIPAA Identifiers

  • Names
  • Geographic subdivisions smaller than state
  • Dates (except year)
  • Telephone numbers
  • Fax numbers
  • Email addresses
  • Social Security numbers
  • Medical record numbers
  • Health plan beneficiary numbers
  • Account numbers
  • Certificate/license numbers
  • Vehicle identifiers and serial numbers
  • Device identifiers and serial numbers
  • Web URLs
  • IP addresses
  • Biometric identifiers
  • Full face photos
  • Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code

Using PHI for Research

Covered entities may use or disclose PHI for research without individual authorization through these pathways:

1. Authorization

Written authorization from the individual specifying what information will be used/disclosed and for what purpose.

2. Waiver of Authorization

IRB or Privacy Board grants waiver if criteria are met (minimal risk to privacy, research could not practicably be conducted without waiver, etc.).

3. De-identification

Data with all 18 identifiers removed (Safe Harbor method) or expert determination that risk of re-identification is very small.

4. Limited Data Set

PHI with certain identifiers removed, used under data use agreement. Can retain dates, city, state, ZIP, and ages.

5. Decedents

PHI of decedents may be used for research about deceased individuals.

6. Preparatory to Research

Limited PHI use to prepare research protocol or assess feasibility, without removing data.

Key Requirements

Minimum Necessary

When using or disclosing PHI, covered entities must make reasonable efforts to limit PHI to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose.

Notice of Privacy Practices

Covered entities must provide individuals with notice of privacy practices, including how PHI may be used for research.

Individual Rights

  • Right to access PHI
  • Right to request amendment
  • Right to accounting of disclosures
  • Right to request restrictions (covered entities may decline)
