General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

European Union

EU Regulation 2016/679: Protection of Personal Data

Authority:European Union
Effective Date:May 25, 2018
Official Source:GDPR Info

Overview

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is a comprehensive data protection law that regulates the processing of personal data of individuals within the European Union and European Economic Area. It applies to any organization processing personal data of EU residents, regardless of the organization's location.

For research, GDPR provides specific provisions (Article 89) that allow derogations from certain rights when processing is for scientific research purposes and appropriate safeguards are in place.

Key Principles

1. Lawfulness, Fairness, Transparency

Processing must be lawful, fair, and transparent to the data subject.

2. Purpose Limitation

Data collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes. Further processing for research purposes is permitted with safeguards.

3. Data Minimization

Only collect data adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary.

4. Accuracy

Data must be accurate and kept up to date.

5. Storage Limitation

Keep data only as long as necessary (exception for research purposes with safeguards).

6. Integrity and Confidentiality

Ensure appropriate security of personal data.

7. Accountability

Controller responsible for and able to demonstrate compliance.

Lawful Bases for Research

At least one of these legal bases must apply:

  • Consent: Freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of agreement
  • Public Interest: Processing necessary for scientific or historical research purposes
  • Legitimate Interests: Pursued by controller or third party (with balancing test)
Note: For special category data (health data, genetic data, etc.), additional conditions must be met under Article 9.

Data Subject Rights

  • Right to be Informed: Transparent information about processing
  • Right of Access: Obtain confirmation of processing and access to data
  • Right to Rectification: Correct inaccurate data
  • Right to Erasure: "Right to be forgotten" (exceptions for research)
  • Right to Restrict Processing: Limit processing in certain circumstances
  • Right to Data Portability: Receive and transfer data
  • Right to Object: Object to processing (exemptions for research)
  • Rights Related to Automated Decision-Making: Not be subject to solely automated decisions

Article 89 permits exemptions from some rights when processing is for scientific research and appropriate safeguards are in place.

Key Requirements for Research

Privacy by Design and by Default

Implement technical and organizational measures to ensure data protection principles are met.

Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)

Required when processing is likely to result in high risk to rights and freedoms. Most research involving health data requires DPIA.

Data Breach Notification

  • Notify supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of breach
  • Notify affected individuals when high risk to their rights and freedoms

Data Protection Officer (DPO)

Required for public authorities or when core activities involve large-scale processing of special categories of data or systematic monitoring.

International Data Transfers

Special requirements for transferring personal data outside the EU/EEA (adequacy decisions, standard contractual clauses, etc.).
