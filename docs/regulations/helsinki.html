Declaration of Helsinki

International

Ethical Principles for Medical Research Involving Human Subjects

Authority:World Medical Association (WMA)
Adopted:1964, Last Amended: 2013
Official Source:WMA Website

Overview

The Declaration of Helsinki, developed by the World Medical Association, is a statement of ethical principles for medical research involving human subjects. First adopted in 1964, it has been amended multiple times and serves as a foundational document in research ethics worldwide.

The Declaration has influenced national and international research regulations, including ICH-GCP guidelines and many country-specific requirements.

General Principles

  • The health of the patient/research subject is the physician's first consideration
  • It is the duty of physicians to promote and safeguard health, well-being, and rights of patients and research subjects
  • Medical progress is based on research that must rest on tests involving human subjects
  • The primary purpose of medical research is to generate new knowledge; this goal can never take precedence over rights and interests of research subjects
  • It is the duty of physicians involved in research to protect life, health, dignity, integrity, right to self-determination, privacy, and confidentiality
  • Research should only be conducted if importance of objective outweighs risks and burdens to research subjects

Key Requirements

Informed Consent

  • Participation must be voluntary with appropriate informed consent
  • Consent must be obtained from research subject or legally authorized representative
  • Information must be given about aims, methods, sources of funding, conflicts of interest, institutional affiliations, anticipated benefits, risks, and discomforts
  • Subject must be informed of right to refuse or withdraw consent at any time

Research Ethics Committees

  • Research protocol must be submitted to research ethics committee for review before study begins
  • Committee must be transparent, independent, and appropriately qualified
  • Committee must consider laws and regulations of country where research is conducted
  • Committee has right to monitor ongoing trials

Privacy and Confidentiality

  • Every precaution must be taken to protect privacy and confidentiality
  • Identifiable human material and data must be stored securely

Vulnerable Groups and Individuals

  • Special attention for those who cannot give or refuse consent themselves
  • Research with vulnerable groups justified only if responsive to health needs of group
  • Group should stand to benefit from knowledge gained

Research in Resource-Limited Settings

The Declaration addresses research in populations and communities with limited resources:

  • Research is justified only if responsive to health needs and priorities
  • Research should have reasonable likelihood of benefiting population
  • Post-trial provisions must be described in protocol and approved by ethics committee
